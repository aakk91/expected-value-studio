How LabRats reads your results

A game result is one measurement of one task on one day. Here is how the app turns those into something you can trust a little more.

Each game keeps its own unit

Reaction is read in milliseconds, Digit span in digits, Stroop in correct answers and answer time. The app never adds them into one brain score. Your five-skill chart on the You tab is a summary of game scores, not an ability rating.

Compared with you, not with others

Your usual range comes from your own earlier runs of the same game, with the same rules and level. A run from an older version of a game, or another difficulty, is kept apart. Until you have five earlier runs, the app doesn't call anything usual.

Clear means larger than your daily spread

People vary from day to day. A change only reads as clear when it is large next to that spread. Weekly patterns, such as slower reactions after short nights, appear only after they hold two weeks running. Before that the app says Not clear yet.

Practice counts too

You get better at a game by playing it. The app looks for that learning curve and allows for it when it compares periods, and alternating periods in an experiment help separate learning from a real change.

What it won't do

LabRats is not a medical device, diagnostic test or treatment. It does not recommend substances or doses, say whether a supplement works for you, or judge fitness for work. A pattern in your results can have many causes, and the app never names one as the cause.

30 short brain games, each measuring one thing

Every game in LabRats is short, timed and scored on what it actually measures: milliseconds for reaction, items for memory span, correct answers for attention. Practice holds all 30.

By Amine Kara, maker of LabRats · Updated 27 November 2026

The games

SkillGames
SpeedReaction, Sound reaction, Symbol match, Connect, Stopwatch, Beat keeper, Go / No-Go, Shapes & colors, Stroop, Flanker, Find the odd one
MemoryN-back memory, Digit span, Corsi blocks, Remember tomorrow
ReasoningRulebreaker, Marbles
SpatialStacks, 3D objects
MultitaskPsychomotor, Airways
Other skillsSame or not (checking), Quick sums (arithmetic), Street scene (scene memory), Fold it (shape folding), Which way (map reading), Sling (launch timing), Tune (pitch matching), Balloon (risk and reward), Slop or Not? (spotting AI images)

Most runs last between 30 seconds and two minutes. Marbles and Street scene take a little longer.

Three ways to play

The daily check-in on Today picks a few games and rotates them so each skill comes back. Practice lets you play any game. Ranked deals you a game and an opponent who played the same items: you win, lose or draw, and your Elo moves per skill.

Free and Premium

Free players get one game per skill each day in Practice plus one extra, five Ranked duels a day, and the full daily check-in. A game you have never played is always free to try. Premium removes those limits and the ads, keeps your whole history and adds the full assessment.

Will it make you smarter?

You will get better at the games, because practice works. Research has not shown that this carries over to general intelligence, and LabRats does not claim it does. What the games are good for is a consistent measure you can repeat, so a change in your day can show up in your numbers.

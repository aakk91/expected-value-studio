Track your reaction time on iPhone

Reaction in LabRats is ten lights. Tap as soon as each one appears. The result is your typical correct response time in milliseconds, plus anything that went wrong along the way.

By Amine Kara, maker of LabRats · Updated 27 November 2026

Try the seven-morning reaction challenge

Play it

  1. Open Practice and choose Reaction, or play it in your daily check-in.
  2. Hold the phone the way you usually do. Tap the moment a light appears. The waits are random so you can't anticipate them.
  3. The result shows your typical response, how it sits against your usual range, and every light on one line. A run takes about 30 seconds.

Sound reaction is the same game with a tone instead of a light.

What the number means

The headline is the median of your correct responses, so one slow light doesn't drag it. A tap before the light comes on counts as an early tap, and a light you miss counts against the score instead of being dropped.

Screen and touch delay differ between phones, so compare your runs on the same phone rather than with someone else's number.

Follow it over time

Your game page shows every run, your usual range, and your first five runs against your last five. With your sleep and caffeine logged, it can also tell you whether your mornings or short nights look different, once there are enough days of each.

The same sample history read 342 ms over the first five days and 320 ms over the last five. The app still calls it No clear change yet, because the gap sits inside that player's daily spread.

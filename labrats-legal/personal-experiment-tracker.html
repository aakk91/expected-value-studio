Run a personal experiment on yourself

A personal experiment in LabRats is a plan: a change, periods with and without it, and the games that will measure it. When the plan ends you get a result for each measurement, or a plain Not clear yet.

By Amine Kara, maker of LabRats · Updated 27 November 2026

Set one up

  1. Open the Lab tab, then Experiments, and tap New.
  2. Name it and write the question in one sentence, for example Does a morning walk change my reaction time?
  3. The default plan runs without it, then with it. Change plan offers the opposite order or alternating twice, and the length of each period.
  4. From then on, your check-in includes the experiment's games and Today shows which period you're in.

On a product you already log, Test this on its sheet starts an experiment with sensible defaults.

How the result reads

At the end, the result card leads with the clearest change, in its own words and unit: 16 ms faster, two more items remembered, more mistakes under conflict. Each measurement gets a strip of dots, one per day, so you can see the spread behind the number. A speed gain that came with more errors is called out as a trade-off.

A measurement only reads as a clear change when the gap is large compared with your day-to-day spread. Otherwise it says unclear. That happens a lot, and it is still useful.

What N-of-1 means here

An N-of-1 trial compares conditions within one person over time. LabRats follows the simple version: planned periods, the same games, a fixed reading at the end. It is not blinded, so expectations can play a part. Learning effects are real too, which is why alternating periods helps.

Do it with friends

Any personal plan can become a Shared Lab: same games, same periods, a group chat, and a group result once five members publish theirs. Joining is free. Hosting a Shared Lab needs Premium.

Medication changes need your clinician's guidance. The app says this in the experiment screen too.

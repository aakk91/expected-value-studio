AFOQT Table Reading, Instrument Comprehension and Block Counting practice

Table Reading. A table has X values across the top and Y values down the side. Each question gives an X and a Y; you find the number where the column and the row meet. Try it on this small table.

Table Reading: which number is at X = +1, Y = −1?

34. Go to the column headed +1, then down to the row labelled −1. The frequent error is to read the row first and land on X = −1, Y = +1, which gives 32.

Instrument Comprehension. Each question shows an artificial horizon and a compass, then several aircraft; you choose the aircraft flying as the instruments say. The published practice pamphlet gives the rules: the aircraft symbol is fixed; when it sits above the horizon line the aircraft is climbing, below it the aircraft is diving; when the pointer is left of zero the aircraft is banked to the pilot’s right; the compass gives the heading, and in the pictures you always look north, with east on your right.

Instrument Comprehension: the horizon line is below the aircraft symbol, the pointer is right of zero and the compass reads W. What is the aircraft doing?

Climbing, banked to the pilot’s left, heading west. Symbol above the horizon line means a climb; pointer right of zero means a left bank; a west heading means the aircraft in the picture points to your left, since you look north.

Block Counting. A pile of identical blocks is drawn in three dimensions, with some blocks numbered. For each numbered block you count how many other blocks touch it. Two blocks touch when their faces meet, fully or in part, including blocks hidden behind or underneath; blocks that meet only along an edge or at a corner do not count.

Block Counting: three identical long blocks lie side by side. Three more lie side by side across them at right angles, covering the same square. How many blocks touch the middle block of the upper layer?

Five. Both neighbors in its own layer touch its long sides. Each of the three lower blocks crosses under part of it and touches its bottom face. Count each block once.

Verbal Analogies: PILOT is to COCKPIT as SURGEON is to: scalpel, hospital, operating room or patient?

Operating room. A cockpit is the specific place where a pilot does the work; the operating room is that place for a surgeon. A hospital is too broad, as an airport would be for the pilot.

Arithmetic Reasoning: an aircraft burns 480 pounds of fuel in 40 minutes. At the same rate, how much does it burn in 1 hour 30 minutes?

1,080 pounds. 480 ÷ 40 = 12 pounds per minute, and 90 minutes × 12 = 1,080.

Math Knowledge: a right triangle has legs of 9 and 12. How long is the hypotenuse?

15. 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, and √225 = 15. It is the 3-4-5 triangle multiplied by 3.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the AFOQT?

The current AFOQT has 516 questions in 12 subtests and takes about five hours with breaks. Ten subtests are cognitive (260 questions in 123 minutes); the Situational Judgment Test has 16 questions and the Self-Description Inventory has 240 statements and is not graded.

How fast is AFOQT Table Reading?

Table Reading has 40 questions in 7 minutes, about 10 seconds per question. Each question gives an X and a Y value and asks for the number where that column and row meet.

Can I try RCMP-style questions with answers?

The first example is the one the RCMP guide itself uses for level 2 numerical questions; the others are original.

Numerical skills, level 2: 2 + 2 × 1 = ?

4. Multiplication comes before addition: 2 × 1 = 2, then 2 + 2 = 4. Working left to right gives the same number here, but not in 2 + 2 × 3, which is 8 and not 12.

Language comprehension: CAUTIOUS is to RECKLESS as GENEROUS is to: kind, stingy, wealthy or polite?

Stingy. Cautious and reckless are opposites, so the answer is the opposite of generous. If you do not know a word, “I don’t know” is an available answer in the real assessment.

Business reasoning: “Every report filed after 5 p.m. is reviewed the next day. Report K was reviewed the next day.” Was report K filed after 5 p.m.? Yes, It cannot be determined, or No?

It cannot be determined. The rule says what happens to late reports. It does not say that only late reports are reviewed the next day, so report K may have been filed earlier.

RCMP memory quotient practice: remember a short notice

Open the notice and read it for up to one minute. Close it, then give yourself 45 seconds for both questions together.

Open or close the study notice

The community hall opens at 9:15 on Tuesday. Sign-in moves to the west entrance for that day. The afternoon workshop still starts at 2:30.

1. Which entrance is being used for sign-in? 2. When does the workshop begin?

Check both answers

The west entrance and 2:30 p.m. The 9:15 time belongs to the opening of the hall. Matching each time to an event helps you recall the second detail.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the RCMP online entrance assessment?

The RCMP guide says approximately 50 to 70 minutes. Timed parts: language comprehension 2 × 5 minutes, numerical skills 2 × 3 minutes, spatial skills 3 + 3 + 4 + 5 minutes, memory quotient about 25 minutes and business reasoning 20 to 25 minutes.

What is the memory quotient section?

You study an image, a list of numbers or a text for 30 seconds or one minute. It then disappears and you answer questions about it, with a shared 45 seconds for the questions in the guide’s weather-report example.

Can I use a calculator?

No. The RCMP guide allows pen and paper only for the numerical section.
